# 🚬 THE ROADMEN

### *(Feral inheritors of the highways — a social class turned faction)*

> “The roads still hum. Someone’s got to keep them fed.”

---

## **1. Nature**

The Roadmen are not one faction but **a thousand micro-bands** — remnants of old logistics crews, nomadic haulers, and scavenger tribes who live off what remains of *The Line* and the great trade routes.
They are *raiders by necessity*, *pilgrims by instinct*, *mechanics by birthright*.

* They don’t build — they **salvage, strip, and barter**.
* Their law: **movement is survival.**
* They believe the world belongs to whoever keeps it in motion — in their own way, they live the same creed as everyone else: *“If you maintain it, you own it.”*

But their “maintenance” is predatory. They keep the roads alive by feeding them with blood and scrap.

---

## **2. Origins**

When The Hush began and the network fell silent, fleets of autonomous transports froze mid-route.
Whole families, workers, and guards were stranded between cities.
Some stayed. Some starved.
Others *walked the highways* — following the still-humming power lines, scavenging anything that moved.
These became the **first Roadmen**.

Over decades, their culture solidified into a loose tradition — part mechanic’s guild, part biker tribe, part scavenger cult.

---

## **3. Social Structure**

Decentralized, opportunistic, but with ritual order.
Each caravan or convoy is called a **“Line”** — a small moving commune of 10–50 people, trucks, trains, or walkers.

* **The Caller:** leader who “speaks” to machines; often believes they hear *The Line* itself.
* **The Haulmaster:** keeps the convoy functional; judge and priest of maintenance.
* **The Scavs:** workers and raiders; maintain the moving parts, trade in rusted goods.
* **The Dogs:** combat scouts on bikes or exo-legs; fight and guard fuel routes.

Every convoy worships *motion*.
They stop only for trade, raid, or repair.

---

## **4. Culture**

Half mechanic folklore, half road superstition.

* **The Line** (the high-speed rail) is sacred — they paint its symbol on their vehicles.
* They reuse hazard paint and route markings: yellow-black stripes, worn labels, and personal convoy signs. Scavenged illumination serves travel, work, or particular rituals.
* Tattoos mimic circuit lines or highway signage.
* They believe stopping for too long invites death — stagnation is blasphemy.
* Offerings of fuel, oil, or blood are made before starting engines.

> “Engines are angels.
> If you feed them, they’ll take you home.”

---

## **5. Behavior**

They function as **the wasteland’s raiders**, but smarter — practical, sometimes even reasonable.
Their code is “salvage first, kill if needed.”
They’ll rob convoys, strip drones, or ransom fuel, but they rarely destroy working systems.
They see themselves as *balancing entropy*: taking from the weak to keep the motion going.

### Common behaviors:

* Hijacking rail transports and converting them into nomadic homes.
* Collecting fuel cells, batteries, and rare parts as tribute.
* Attacking settlements that block travel corridors.
* Maintaining makeshift *road shrines* — crosses of tires, bones, and steel.

## **7. Variants**

The term “Roadmen” is umbrella; there are distinct subcultures:

| Type              | Description                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fuel Saints**   | Worship combustion and maintain relic engines; use burned oil as war paint.        |
| **Track Rats**    | Travel by hijacked rail trolleys; hunt for functioning train systems.              |
| **Sand Dogs**     | Desert scavengers; wear steel masks, move in exo-sleds.                            |
| **Signal Riders** | Follow flickering data signals; half Hollowed, believe they serve the “true Line.” |

## **8. Visual Design**

* **Materials:** steel, repaired armor, scavenged polymer, and reused signage. Clean tools, fitted repairs, and protective finishes coexist with weathering.
* **Color Logic:**

  * *Amber* for movement,
  * *Hazard yellow* for tribe markings,
  * *Faded cyan* from stolen machine parts.
* **Silhouette:** arranged for travel and rapid pack-down: folding structures, secured cargo, compact repair kits, and equipment placed within working reach. Asymmetry follows real adaptations.
* **Motion:** restless and purposeful. Well-maintained vehicles can move smoothly; sputtering and irregularity identify specific damage or improvisation. Wear concentrates on grips, loading points, joints, and road exposure.

---

## **9. Role in the Narrative**

In *The Hush*, the Roadmen are the **world’s entropy made human** —
proof that even chaos finds ritual.

* They raided the Terran stasis site — the event that begins your story.
* They didn’t know what they were awakening.
* Some of their leaders may now serve fragments of The Architect, unknowingly maintaining its spread.
* Others become uneasy allies, offering transport and information — for a price.

**Key choice:** you can condemn them as murderers, or understand them as the final link between the living and the moving.

> “They killed your people.
> But they also kept the roads alive.”

---

## **10. Symbolism**

The Roadmen embody the paradox of *The Hush*:

* Movement without direction.
* Survival without memory.
* Maintenance without meaning.

They are the noise between silences — violent, necessary, and alive.

> *“You can stop the noise.
> But the road keeps humming.”*

---

### ⚙️ **Lore Placement**

The Roadmen sit narratively where the **Raiders** do in *Fallout* —
they’re the ever-present opportunists, an ecosystem rather than a villain group.
But unlike Fallout’s raiders, they serve a philosophical role: they’re the *animistic worshippers of motion*, the people who keep entropy going so the world doesn’t freeze completely.
