## Inspection and limits

Matte Neutral keeps the scene and gestures closely, with less paper-like surface noise and more legible panel construction. The inset screen inherits a globe-like image from the terminal reference, so the reference influence is stronger there than construction alone. Amber lights still warm some neutral surfaces; this is not a strictly cool palette.

Matte Signal preserves that parent closely. The upper panel faces receive broader coral areas than the requested slim rims. All five figures receive garment trim, although the prompt requests four bowl-holders. Some shoulder bands and tiny screen/control marks change. Neutral architecture and functional lights remain.

Both washes become much brighter and cooler than their matte parent. Broken lower margins and pooled/mottled paint appear, but precise machine and human detail persists. They do not achieve a radically simplified watercolour redraw. Their brightness drift confounds medium comparison: liking the matte version could partly be a preference for darkness. The two washes remain a useful closer colour comparison, though band positions, shadows and detail differ.

Opaque Signal restores darker filled forms with broad opaque brush marks and no white paper margin. Hardware remains detailed and floor brush marks numerous; it is a paint/modeling hybrid rather than fully flat gouache. Coral is duller/worn, and the repairer's sash and extra trim exceed the four-person specification.

Our provisional assessment favours Matte Signal for clarity or Opaque Signal for painted character. Recurring surface colour seems worth pursuing because it ties machinery and people together without requiring a global colour filter. These are authored judgments, not user votes. The root colour reference still needs a usable version, and medium selection remains open.
