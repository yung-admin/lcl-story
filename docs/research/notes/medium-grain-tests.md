## Studies and actual results

| Study | Style hypothesis | Inspected result and comparison limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Grain](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-01-grain.png) | Analogue granular finish on the existing opaque painting | Fine irregular grain across floor, masonry, cream casings and clothing. Source modeling, palette/value distribution and some faceted patches remain. Closest finish-only comparison; less forceful at small scale than the print/raster studies. |
| [Drybrush](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-02-drybrush.png) | Broad dry opaque gouache and visible surface tooth | Targeted refinement replaces much faceted noise with larger directional strokes and cut cream value planes. Clearer painting departure; still volumetric, with some detailed modeling. Board tooth is less visible than brush direction. Amber strokes and midtones become broader/brighter. |
| [Impasto](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-03-oil.png) | Thick oil/knife paint | Raised ridges and chunky plates strongly change the world surface. Often reads as embossed/cracked tessellation over retained modeling rather than broadly composed oil strokes. Local highlights/reflections become stronger. |
| [Mezzotint](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-04-mezzotint.png) | Coloured velvety print/charcoal grain and scraped highlights | Denser coarse stipple and scraped-looking light; hue family and darker mood stay close. Converges toward a heavily grained painting, with original geometry retained. Some texture reads as surface pitting. This is an inspired raster hypothesis, not a physical print process. |
| [Screenprint](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-05-screenprint.png) | Large cut ink shapes with selective coarse screens | Targeted refinement adds solid petrol floor/shadow fields and cream faces, grouped details and selective halftone transitions. Strong graphic departure. Cream and yellow-amber are brighter, coral/green more saturated and contrast more poster-like than the softer control, despite the palette/mood lock request. |
| [Dither](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-06-dither.png) | Coarse quantized raster world against modern graphic HUD | Clear stepped silhouettes and clustered raster shading across figures, floor and machines. Clusters are mixed/stochastic rather than uniformly ordered. Hue family remains, but hard cream/amber transitions increase perceived contrast; small details simplify or disappear. |

Five people, four serving bowls/trays and principal camera/layout anchors remain in every final. Required HUD wording and 01–04 tabs remain legible. Fine fittings, clothing marks and world edges change with the medium. These are not pixel-identical edits or an implemented renderer. Screenprint's increased brightness/saturation and Drybrush's broader amber strokes mean the comparison does not isolate medium from every value change.

Initial [Drybrush](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-02-drybrush-draft.png) clung to modeled facets. Initial [Screenprint](../../../images/style-lab/round-10/r10-05-screenprint-draft.png) placed dots nearly everywhere over retained shading. Each received one native refinement to change drawing language more substantially; drafts remain outside the gallery.

## Authored assessment and next questions

Drybrush is the stronger candidate for a more visibly authored painting while retaining the familiar world. Grain is the closest mood-preserving finish option. Dither offers the sharpest old-raster/new-HUD contrast, and Screenprint offers a bolder graphic construction, with its brighter contrast requiring separate judgment. Impasto's embossed quality and Mezzotint's convergence with Grain are useful limits rather than reasons to label them accomplished oil/print pipelines.

The proposed review was to judge the whole image first, then separate medium, grain strength and drawing edges. A preferred texture may be transferable to another treatment. Neutral labels are not acceptance; subsequent direct feedback is recorded below.
