## What this suggests

Our authored assessment is that **precise information embedded in a heavy painted body** is a useful identity direction. Recesses, protected readouts, mechanical counters and selectors give the sharp graphics a physical home. Motion and open access should explain state alongside light and text. The service hatch reads more immediately than the tiny selector; subsequent exploration should strengthen physical pointers and test that distinction without relying on colour alone.

Service Spine suggests an architectural vocabulary of rectangular access routes around locally rounded machines. The system feels maintained and connected. Its extra detail and lamps are worth judging separately: the simpler Synthesis remains the control for whether this build-out improves the scene.

The counter decrement, nozzle operation, local selector and service lever are proposed visual behaviours. They do not establish allocation rules, a new override mechanic, machine consciousness or canon. No final engine, medium, motion pipeline or interface implementation has been chosen. This one hall does not impose a universal night palette or shape rule.
